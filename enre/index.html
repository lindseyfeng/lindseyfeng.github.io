ENRE LMP Backtest

The INFORMS ENRE 2026–27 challenge asks for a 48-hour day-ahead LMP forecast with a 90% interval. This dashboard covers the baselines' rolling-origin backtest. Each forecast day o uses prices and fuel through o, plus load/solar for o+1 and o+2. Extreme hours follow the challenge's definition: above the 95th or below the 5th percentile of the preceding week.

90% interval

How the baseline intervals are built. Each model gets its own interval, using empirical residual quantiles (split conformal) kept separately for every (lead, hour) cell. At forecast day o, take that model's errors actual − forecast from the previous 91 forecast days. Origin o−1 is skipped, because its day-2 target is not realized yet. The 5th and 95th percentiles of those 91 errors are added to today's point forecast to give lo and hi. The first 28 days with too few past errors are dropped. No future information enters: the interval is built only from errors that were observable at o.

Coverage @ 90 is the share of hours in the window where lo ≤ actual ≤ hi (target 0.90). Winkler is (hi − lo) + 20·(lo − actual) when the actual falls below the band, + 20·(actual − hi) when above, and the width alone otherwise. It is averaged over hours, and lower is better. For an uploaded CSV, both are computed from its <model>_lo / _hi columns. Models without them are left out of this section.

CSV format. One row per forecast hour. Required columns: actual, hour, and either origin + lead or target_day. Every other numeric column is read as a model's point forecast. <model>_lo / <model>_hi give its 90% interval. ext_lo / ext_hi give the extreme thresholds; without them they are computed from the file's own actuals. A file you load is parsed in your browser; it is uploaded only if you press Save this run. Baselines and code: Naive, Naive-week, Heat-rate ARX, LEAR (Lago et al. 2021), GBM, and the LEAR+GBM average, with conformal intervals.
